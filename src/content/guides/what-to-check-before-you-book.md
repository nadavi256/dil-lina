A design hotel is very good at the first three seconds. Stone, a lamp, a plant, a bed in a pool of light. The booking page is where that picture either holds or falls apart. Run this list before you pay. It is the same list Lina uses before a rate is allowed out of the TODO state.

## The total, not the teaser

Look at the price for your dates, for your number of guests, after taxes and fees. In many European cities a stay tax is added on top and is easy to miss until the last screen. If breakfast is a line item, decide whether you want it. A hotel can look inexpensive because breakfast was stripped out, or expensive because it was forced in.

Write down the figure you are actually willing to pay. If you are using Lina’s brief, that number is often somewhere around $80 to $150 for the night, for two. It is a budget, not a promise that the hotel in front of you will hit it.

## Which room the price belongs to

Beautiful hotels sell the best room in the gallery and price a smaller one in the search result. Open the specific room. Check the bed: a “double” is sometimes two twins pushed together, and sometimes a bed that is generous. Check whether the window faces the courtyard or a shaft. If the listing gives a size in square meters, believe a small number. Twenty square meters with a decent bathroom can be a pleasure. Twelve, with the bed against the door, will not feel like the photograph.

## The cancellation sentence

Read the rate name. Flexible and non-refundable are different purchases. A lower non-refundable rate is only a deal if your dates are firm. If you are still choosing between [Paris](/dil-lina/cities/paris/), [Rome](/dil-lina/cities/rome/), and [Barcelona](/dil-lina/cities/barcelona/), pay for the right to change your mind, or wait until you are sure.

## The walk, in minutes you trust

“City center” is a marketing area, not a distance. Look at the map pin against the place you actually want to be in the evening, when you are tired. A fifteen-minute walk to dinner is a feature. A thirty-minute walk with luggage from the metro, uphill, is the thing people complain about later. The hotel’s own directions are useful. So is dropping the pin into the map you already use.

## Recent words from guests, on the booking site

Lina does not reprint guest scores. A single number ages badly, and copying it into an article makes it look like a fact we measured. On Booking.com, read the newest reviews and the middling ones. Look for notes about noise, heat, and the difference between the photograph and the room. A hotel can be wonderful and still wrong for a light sleeper on a Saturday.

## Then book in the open

Use the hotel’s page on Booking.com, including the buttons on this site. Those buttons are affiliate links. If you book, Lina may earn a commission, and that commission is not added to your rate. The [affiliate disclosure](/dil-lina/disclosure/) says this in full. If you would rather book on the hotel’s own site after you have compared, do that. A good hotel note should survive the click.

When a rate on Lina eventually leaves the TODO state, it will name the room, the occupancy, the night, and the day it was checked. Anything short of that is a story, not a price.
